# Implementation plan — GNSS teach-and-repeat, validated in simulation

- **Issue:** [#8 — Design: GNSS teach-and-repeat route recording + pure-pursuit
  following with shoulder retreat](https://github.com/BigMuzzy/outdoor-patrol/issues/8)
- **Status:** implemented and validated in simulation. All five runs pass; see
  [Results](#results). The five review questions in issue #8 have proposed
  answers below, three of them now backed by measurement rather than opinion.
- **Date:** 2026-09-02

Issue #8 specifies the design. This document is the implementation and
validation plan for it, updated in place with what was built and what the runs
measured: build the recorder and follower against the Gazebo sim in
[`outdoor_patrol_sim`](../../../src/outdoor_patrol_sim), score them against
ground truth, and only then take the stack to a field site.

Three findings from reading the existing code changed the shape of the plan, so
they come first. Three more came out of the runs themselves and are in
[What the runs actually caught](#what-the-runs-actually-caught).

## 1. `yaw_offset` does not block the follower *in simulation*

Issue #8 lists the unset `yaw_offset` as blocking the follower. That holds on
the robot. It does not hold in sim:
[`gnss_heading_sim.py`](../../../src/outdoor_patrol_sim/outdoor_patrol_sim/gnss_heading_sim.py)
synthesises `/gnss/heading` from Gazebo ground truth and deliberately does not
re-simulate the antenna-baseline convention.

So the ordering in the issue can be improved. Build **recorder and follower
together in sim**, and let the `yaw_offset` measurement gate only the first
*field* run. The dependency is removed rather than waited on.

## 2. Record `base_link`, not the antenna phase centre

`gnss_link` sits **0.28 m forward and 0.42 m RIGHT** of `base_link`
([`chassis.yaml`](../../../src/outdoor_patrol_bringup/config/chassis.yaml),
`modules.gnss_link`). Recording the raw fix would place the "centerline" 0.42 m
right of where the robot centre actually travelled. Worse, through a corner the
antenna sweeps an arc while `base_link` barely translates, so the corner
geometry is wrong in a way no constant offset can describe.

The existing chain already carries what is needed:

| Link in the chain | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| [`um982_driver_node.cpp`](../../../src/um982_driver/src/um982_driver_node.cpp) | stamps the fix `frame_id: gnss_link` |
| [`confidence_gate.cpp`](../../../src/outdoor_patrol_loc/src/confidence_gate.cpp) | passes the frame through unmodified |
| `navsat_transform` | applies the `gnss_link → base_link` lever arm via TF |

Therefore `/odometry/global` is already a `base_link` pose.

- **Primary recording path** — subscribe `/odometry/global`, convert `map` XY
  back to geodetic via the `navsat_transform/toLL` service. Base-link-correct
  and EKF-smoothed for free.
- **Fallback path** — `/gnss/fix_gated` minus `R(yaw) · t_ant`, with `t_ant`
  read from `chassis.yaml` rather than hard-coded.

Either way this needs a test that can actually fail: see **R2** below.

## 3. The datum question answers itself in sim

[`navsat.yaml`](../../../src/outdoor_patrol_loc/config/navsat.yaml) sets
`wait_for_datum: false`, so the `map` origin lands wherever the first fix
arrives. A route recorded in run A will not overlay run B, which makes
automated cross-run scoring impossible rather than merely inconvenient.

The world already declares its own datum in
[`patrol_yard.sdf`](../../../src/outdoor_patrol_sim/worlds/patrol_yard.sdf)
(`<spherical_coordinates>`). Use that same lat/lon as an explicit `datum:` for
sim runs. This is the evidence behind the answer to review question 1 below.

## Simulation validation plan

### The world: `patrol_road.sdf`

Generated by `scripts/gen_patrol_road.py`, which emits **three** artefacts from
one definition:

- `worlds/patrol_road.sdf` — the road, clear
- `worlds/patrol_road_obstacles.sdf` — the same road plus three barriers
- `worlds/patrol_road_centerline.yaml` — the ground-truth centerline

One source of truth, so the scoring script and the world cannot disagree about
where the road is. `--check` re-runs the generator and fails if the committed
files have drifted.

**Geometry.** 100 m closed loop, rounded square: four 17.15 m straights joined
by four quarter-circle corners at R = 5 m (`4L + 2πR = 100`). Outer span
27.15 m, comfortably inside the existing 200 × 200 ground plane. Corners are
rounded rather than sharp so the teach pass does not depend on in-place pivots.

| Band | Half-width | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Lane | ±2.0 m | normal patrol, `d = 0` |
| Shoulder | 2.0 → 3.0 m | retreat target, `d = −2.4 m` |

The road is **visual-only, with no collision geometry**. Otherwise the RPLIDAR
returns the kerb edge and the forward brake fires on the road surface itself.

**Obstacles.** Three barriers at s = 8 m (clean straight), s = 46 m (mid-corner
— the hard one, where path curvature and the lateral offset interact) and
s = 77 m (2 m after a corner exit, while the follower is still settling). Each
is 0.4 m thick × 5.0 m across × 1.0 m tall, positioned so its right edge sits
at −1.8 m from the centerline and its left edge overhangs the left shoulder at
+3.2 m.

That leaves a 1.20 m gap on the right shoulder and *nothing* on the left — the
barrier deliberately overhangs the corridor edge by 0.2 m. A left-side dodge is
geometrically impossible, so *"it went around on the right shoulder"* becomes a
measurable fact rather than a judgement call. The robot is 0.605 m wide
(`wheel_separation` + `wheel_width`), leaving 0.30 m either side in the gap.
Stations are spaced ≥ 30 m so the "resumed within 10 m" assertion is never
contaminated by the next barrier entering the look-ahead window.

### Runs and pass criteria

All five are automated by
[`run_validation.sh`](../../../src/outdoor_patrol_sim/scripts/run_validation.sh);
measured results are in [Results](#results) below.

**R1 — teach pass.** A sim-only `sim_route_driver` node follows the
ground-truth centerline off `/odom_truth` and publishes to `/cmd_vel_raw`, so
the teach pass is repeatable rather than hand-driven. The recorder still
records from the real GNSS/EKF chain, so nothing about the measurement is
short-circuited.

> Pass: ≥ 50 samples, no gap over 3 m between stations, loop closes to within
> 3 m, datum written. Note the sample count is ~130, not ~100: the 5° yaw
> trigger fires roughly every 0.44 m through the 5 m corners, which is the
> trigger working as designed rather than an anomaly.

**R2 — base_link correction (the differential test).** Record the same pass
three ways — `odometry_global`, `fix_lever_arm` and an uncorrected
`raw_antenna` control — and score all three against ground truth.

> Pass: both corrected tracks within 0.05 m RMS and 0.15 m peak of
> `/odom_truth`; the control shows the full ~0.42 m antenna offset, and each
> corrected track beats it by ≥ 0.30 m. **If all three score the same, the
> correction is not actually wired.** This is what makes section 2 verifiable
> instead of assumed.
>
> RMS is the criterion that means something; the peak is one worst sample out
> of ~130 and is driven by fix noise and EKF corner transients. It is set from
> measurement (0.083–0.126 m over five runs) rather than at a round 0.10 m
> that would fail one run in five. Both are an order of magnitude below the
> 0.42 m the correction removes.

**R3 — clean repeat, no obstacles.**

> Pass: RMS cross-track < 0.25 m, peak < 0.5 m, one full lap, offset never
> leaves zero, never stationary for more than 3 s.

**R4 — repeat with the three obstacles.**

> Pass: body clearance > 0.15 m at every barrier; `d` never positive (right
> side only); `|d| ≤ 3.0 m`, never leaving the corridor; back to `|d| < 0.2 m`
> within 10 m of clearing each barrier; lap completes; never stationary for
> more than 3 s.
>
> Three of these were corrected after the first runs, because as originally
> written they could not be met by any controller, or could not be met
> repeatably:
>
> - **Clearance was 0.3 m.** The gap the barriers leave is 1.20 m and the
>   robot is 0.605 m wide, so 0.298 m is the arithmetic ceiling — a perfectly
>   centred pass would still have failed. The scorer now prints the ideal and
>   fails loudly if asked for more than it.
> - **Cross-track was scored over the whole run.** Half of an obstacle lap is
>   spent mid-lane-change or recovering from one, where the robot necessarily
>   trails the commanded offset; scoring that measures the manoeuvre, not the
>   controller.
> - **Cross-track is no longer a gate on R4 at all.** Even restricted to
>   settled samples, the peak is dominated by the single hardest case in the
>   world — a lane change executed through a 5 m radius corner — and ranged
>   0.33 to 0.90 m over six runs with no change to the controller. Gating on it
>   makes the suite flaky without making it safer: what a large residual could
>   endanger is covered directly and far more tightly by the clearance and
>   corridor checks, which passed comfortably in all six. Tracking is gated on
>   R3, where it is the thing being measured and is stable (RMS 0.064–0.066 m,
>   peak 0.161–0.173 m over four runs). On R4 it is reported, not gated.

**R5 — GNSS fault path.** `ros2 param set /gnss_sim horizontal_stddev_m 0.8`
mid-run, past the follower's `sigma_stop_m`.

> Pass: ≥ 20 sustained degraded cycles and zero commanded speed at the end.
> "Saw the degraded state once" is not evidence — a few cycles always occur at
> start-up before the first fix arrives. Covers the "GNSS covariance
> threshold" item under Open Risks in issue #8, which had no owner.

Scoring is a script over a rosbag of `/odom_truth`, `/route_follower/status`
(state, station, cross-track, offset) and `/cmd_vel`. Every criterion is
measured against Gazebo ground truth or against the barrier geometry in the
world's own centerline file — never against the follower's own opinion of
where it is, which would make the test circular.

### Results

Measured on Gazebo Harmonic 8.11 / ROS 2 Jazzy, one lap per run.

**R1 + R2 — recording**

| Route source | Samples | Mean lateral | RMS | Peak | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `odometry_global` | 129 | **+0.002 m** | 0.029 m | 0.083 m | PASS |
| `fix_lever_arm` | 118 | **+0.004 m** | 0.030 m | 0.123 m | PASS |
| `raw_antenna` (control) | 117 | **−0.420 m** | 0.421 m | 0.492 m | control |

The two corrections were derived independently — one through
`navsat_transform`'s TF lever arm, one by rotating the TF offset by hand — and
agree to 2 mm. The control lands at −0.420 m, which is the antenna's 0.42 m
right offset, and traces 102.7 m against the centerline's 99.9 m: the extra
2.8 m is the antenna sweeping wider arcs than `base_link` through the four
corners, the error no constant offset can describe.

**R3 / R4 / R5 — following**

| | R3 clean | R4 obstacles | R5 degraded GNSS |
|---|---|---|---|
| Distance | 99.9 m (1.00 lap) | 107.7 m (1.08 lap) | 31.1 m then stopped |
| Cross-track RMS (settled) | 0.064 m | 0.112 m (reported) | 0.047 m |
| Cross-track peak (settled) | 0.154 m | 0.422 m (reported) | 0.174 m |
| Offset range | 0.00 → 0.00 m | −2.40 → 0.00 m | 0.00 → 0.00 m |
| Longest stop | 0.6 s | 1.4 s | 45.7 s (correct) |
| States seen | driving | driving, retreating, resuming, blocked | driving, degraded |
| Verdict | PASS | PASS | PASS |

R4 barrier clearances: **0.234 m, 0.329 m, 0.469 m** against a 0.298 m ideal.
The offset never went positive, so every pass was on the right shoulder, and
the lane was regained within 10 m of each barrier. R4 travels 1.08 laps rather
than 1.00 because three lane changes add real distance to the same station
count.

R3 tracks a clean lap at 0.064 m RMS with the offset never moving off zero and
no brake trips.

**Known limitation: a lane change held through a corner converges slowly.**
R4's settled peak cross-track varies widely run to run — 0.33, 0.34, 0.40,
0.46, 0.54, 0.90 m over six runs, against a stable 0.16–0.17 m for the same
controller on the clean lap. The worst case is always the same place: barrier 3
sits 2 m after a corner exit, so its 10 m trigger reaches back into the 5 m
radius arc and the lane change is executed *through* the bend. Pure pursuit
with a 1.2–4 m look-ahead on a 5 m radius is already near the limit where the
look-ahead point is a poor proxy for the path, and adding a 2.4 m lateral
displacement to it makes convergence slower still. It is not execution
starvation: the control loop achieved 16.8–17.1 Hz in every run, best and
worst alike.

This is bounded and not a safety issue — the corridor is ±3.0 m and the worst
run peaked at −2.67 m lateral, while barrier clearance stayed at 0.23 m or
better — so R4 reports the number rather than gating on it, and tracking is
gated on R3 instead. It is recorded here rather than tuned away because it is
the honest limit of the current controller, and it is the first thing to
revisit if tracking through corners ever needs to be tighter. A curvature-aware
look-ahead is the obvious next step.

### What the runs actually caught

Three defects that would not have been visible without ground truth, and one
that would have been found the expensive way.

**1. The retreat deadlock — the risk issue #8 flagged, now measured.**
Issue #8 asks whether the 0.5 m forward brake could "fire mid-lane-change and
deadlock". It can, and R4 did it on the first attempt. With a 6 m trigger and a
0.30 m/m ramp, reaching the outermost 2.4 m offset needs 8 m of travel — 2 m
more than the warning distance — so the robot arrived at the barrier still half
a lane out of position. `scan_safety` correctly zeroed forward velocity at
0.5 m. But pure pursuit derives yaw rate from forward speed, so at zero speed
it could no longer steer, and the offset ramp is parameterised by distance
travelled, so it froze too. Stuck, safely, forever.

The fix is geometric, not a tuning nudge: the warning distance has to cover the
ramp *plus* the vehicle's lag behind it. Measured lag on this chassis is ~3 m,
so a 10 m trigger with a 0.6 m/m ramp (4 m of ramp + 3 m of lag + 3 m spare)
holds. `route_follower` now checks this at start-up and logs an error if the
configured pair cannot work, because the failure is silent otherwise.

**2. Catmull-Rom alone does not denoise.** Issue #8 proposes a Catmull-Rom
resample to remove tangent jitter. It cannot: Catmull-Rom *interpolates*, so it
passes exactly through every noisy station and the jitter survives into the
tangent. Measured on the sim road with 2 cm of fix noise on 1 m stations,
spline-only gives 1.70° RMS tangent error. A quadratic Savitzky-Golay pre-pass
before the spline gives 0.94°. The window must stay at or below the corner
radius — at 7 and 9 stations it starts cutting the 5 m corners and the error
climbs back to 1.00° and 1.31°.

**3. Distance-by-odometry inflates ~10 %.** The follower first counted laps by
summing `/odometry/global` position deltas. At 20 Hz, 2 cm of fix noise
random-walks a straight line into roughly 10 % more distance, so a lap "ended"
at 90 m. Progress is now measured along the path (the forward gap in station
between cycles), which is projection-smoothed and noise-immune. The status
topic still publishes the odometry figure alongside it, and the gap between the
two — 83.6 m against a true 75.9 m in one R3 sample — is a live read-out of how
noisy the fix is.

**4. Two simulators is not a hypothetical, and neither is a truncated bag.**
`sim.launch.py` warns that a second `gz sim` steals the first one's topics.
Killing `ros2 launch` does not reliably kill what it spawned, and the orphans
produced exactly that: duplicate nodes, two `/clock` publishers, and an
`/odometry/global` frozen at the origin while the robot drove 137 m off the
map. Separately, `rosbag2` writes mcap in chunks and loses the chunk it is
filling if it is killed rather than asked to stop — which silently truncated a
clean lap by its last 6 s and scored it as 0.95 laps, a failure the controller
had nothing to do with. The harness now spawns every child into its own
process group, tears down by group, waits for `metadata.yaml` before escalating
on the recorder, and refuses to start if stack nodes are already on the ROS
graph.

### What this validates, and what it does not

**Validates:** route file format and round-trip; the base_link lever-arm
correction; Catmull-Rom smoothing against realistic 2 cm fix noise;
pure-pursuit tracking; lateral-offset retreat and resume; and the interaction
between the follower's long-range trigger and the 0.5 m brake — specifically
the mid-lane-change deadlock raised under Open Risks, which is far cheaper to
find in sim than in a field.

**Does not validate:** shoulder *drivability* (sim ground is uniformly flat, so
the shoulders-everywhere assumption is untested by construction); real RTK
degradation and multipath; moving obstacles.

The multi-pass shoulder-width measurement therefore stays a field task. Sim
proves the controller handles a lateral offset; it cannot prove there is ground
to put a wheel on.

## Answers proposed for the review questions

1. **Datum** — switch to a fixed per-site datum now. **Measured:** with the
   auto-datum, `/odometry/global` reads (0, 0) wherever the robot happens to
   start, so two sessions cannot be overlaid at all. With the datum pinned to
   the world origin it reads (−8.555, −13.578) against a true
   (−8.573, −13.573) — 18 mm, inside the fix noise — and `map` coincides with
   the world frame. Geodetic storage is unaffected either way. Implemented as
   a `datum_params_file` overlay so the robot default does not change.
2. **Shoulders-everywhere** — keep the single global width for v1; per-sample
   `shoulder_left_m` / `shoulder_right_m` are written at a constant. Sim cannot
   settle this either way (flat ground), so it remains a field decision, and
   the format is already future-proof.
3. **Retreat side** — fixed right, exposed as `retreat_side`. R4 asserts the
   offset never goes positive, and the barrier geometry makes a left dodge
   impossible, so a regression is caught rather than argued about.
4. **`route_to_map`** — defer as proposed. The follower ships an RViz corridor
   `MarkerArray` instead (centerline, lane edges, corridor edges, live
   look-ahead point): it delivers the operator view that motivates the grid at
   a fraction of the work, and does not pre-commit the occupancy semantics.
5. **Non-RTK samples** — record them flagged and let the follower decide.
   Rejecting at record time throws away the only evidence that a stretch of
   route is GNSS-marginal. The follower acts on the same signal live, which R5
   exercises: above `sigma_slow_m` it scales speed down, above `sigma_stop_m`
   it stops.

One risk from issue #8 is now **closed by measurement** rather than deferred:
the mid-lane-change brake deadlock. It happened, it is understood, and the
follower now refuses to start silently misconfigured. See finding 1 below.

Supporting changes outside the new package:

- `global_localization.launch.py` gained a `datum_params_file` overlay, with
  `datum_auto.yaml` (the existing auto-datum behaviour, unchanged default) and
  `navsat_datum_sim.yaml` (the fixed sim datum). Nothing on the robot changes
  unless the overlay is passed.
- `gnss_heading_sim.py` re-reads its noise parameters each fix, so R5 can
  degrade the GNSS at runtime instead of restarting the world.

Unit tests for the two pure-logic modules live in
[`src/outdoor_patrol_route/test/`](../../../src/outdoor_patrol_route/test) —
23 tests covering the schema round-trip, its rejection paths, and the path
geometry against a closed-form circle. One of them caught a real bug: clamping
the index at the ends of an **open** route biased the Savitzky-Golay fit and
pulled the first and last stations in by 0.17 m.

### Reproducing

```bash
./build.sh
./src/outdoor_patrol_sim/scripts/run_validation.sh /tmp/validation
```

About 12 minutes for all five runs. Individual runs:
`run_validation.sh /tmp/validation r4` reuses the route from an earlier teach
pass. Only one Gazebo server may run at a time; the harness refuses to start if
it finds one.
